## 一、逐句译文（按编号顺序，数组元素与编号一一对应）

### 第 1 段（11 句）
1. June arrived with a heat wave and a phishing email.
2. On the first Saturday of the month, David walked to the office through a campus that had turned beautiful overnight: the grass was long, a crane stood over the new library, ants moved in a busy line along a brick wall, and a bush by the gate was loud with insects.
3. A warm air pressed down on everything, visible in the shimmer above the road.
4. He was barely awake when he opened the support inbox at midday.
5. The message warned that the account would be closed within twenty-four hours unless the user confirmed their details.
6. It carried the university's mark, a plain blue button, and a file attached to the signature.
7. David nearly clicked it.
8. Something about the greeting was wrong: the university always wrote "Dear student", and the sender's address ended in a string of letters that looked like a knot.
9. He opened the source code and found a form that would send every password to a server in another country.
10. "This is not from us," he said.
11. "And it is not only us."

### 第 2 段（13 句）
12. By the afternoon, the reports were coming in.
13. Students had received the same email, and some had followed it.
14. Their campus card accounts were empty, and the numbers on the bank's screen went downward all day.
15. The money had vanished with amazing speed.
16. A crowd gathered outside the bank on the north road, and a news helicopter circled above the roof.
17. A policeman tried to keep the line calm.
18. David saw a teenager sitting on the steps with her phone in her hands.
19. Her name was Xiao Lin, she was nineteen, and she had begun to weep.
20. She had saved four thousand yuan for her mother's surgery, and the money was gone.
21. "I thought it was the university," she said.
22. "It looked so official." Sarah sat beside her and put a blanket around her shoulders, though the air was warm.
23. Xiao Lin would not let anyone touch her phone.
24. The scene was difficult to watch.

### 第 3 段（9 句）
25. David, Mark, and Sarah worked through the night.
26. Under a table lamp they divided the work: Mark traced the domain, Sarah and an artist friend designed a warning poster, and David read every report.
27. The fake page was a copy of theirs, but the colors were slightly off and the address was wrong.
28. Mark found that the domain had been registered only ten days earlier, and behind it lay a dump of stolen addresses, a list of accounts, and a link to a stock exchange in the south.
29. "This is a professional operation," he said.
30. By midnight they had reached a conclusion, and by morning they were ready.
31. David went to the campus security office, where an official with a calm voice listened to the whole story and asked for proof.
32. "You have done the right thing," he said.
33. "Most people stay passive. You did not."

### 第 4 段（9 句）
34. Xiao Lin's account had been emptied by a man who called her on the phone.
35. He spoke with a foreign accent and said he was from the bank's security department.
36. He urged her to move her cash to a "safe account" for two hours, and he stayed on the line until she had done it.
37. He even gave her an appointment number and promised a certificate of protection.
38. "I did not misunderstand him," she said.
39. "I understood every word. I was just afraid." She was shy, almost timid, and the scammer had used that against her.
40. The scam was simple, and that was its power.
41. A slice of the stolen money had already been spent on a golf membership and a hotel room; the rest had gone through a chain of accounts and disappeared.
42. The police had little to work with, but they had one witness: Xiao Lin remembered the man's voice and the exact time of the call.

### 第 5 段（14 句）
43. The story reached the city newspaper, and a correspondent came to the office to interview David.
44. She was a thorough writer with a warm manner, and she asked the questions nobody else had asked: Who was responsible for the data?
45. Who had sold the addresses?
46. The answers led to a small company in a brick building near the canal, in a quiet neighborhood where the smell of flour drifted from a bakery.
47. The company's reputation was good, and its finance records looked clean, but the trail did not.
48. The owner, an old gentleman, became hostile when they mentioned the acquisition of student data.
49. "They did not steal the passwords," David said.
50. "They bought the list that made the theft possible. It was a spill of personal data onto the open market." The correspondent cited his words in her article, and the story became part of a larger conversation about privacy.
51. A freight train passed the canal in slow motion.
52. A chemistry student had lost a month's allowance, and a vast collection of addresses was still being sold.
53. The university announced a review, and the state promised a reduction in such crimes.
54. It was a preliminary victory.
55. Further questions remained.
56. The relevant records were still missing.

### 第 6 段（11 句）
57. At the end of the week David attended a lecture on trust and fraud.
58. The professor was a conservative philosopher with a dry sense of humor and a simple philosophy: trust is the true currency of an economy, and a democracy depends on the same fragile element.
59. "The myth of the digital world is that it is separate from the real one," he said.
60. "It is not. Every password is a small promise, and every promise can be broken." He drew a contrast between passive users, who wait to be attacked, and active citizens, who learn to see the danger early.
61. A healthy society needs a harmony of trust and law, and no country can import trust.
62. "The ideal citizen does not need a radar to notice a storm," he said.
63. "You need to look at the sky." He went further, comparing local rules with federal ones in other countries, and said that everything depended on the first small check.
64. The lecture was part of a campaign to combat fraud.
65. David wrote the sentence in his notebook and underlined it.
66. The lecture was not boring; it was the most interesting hour of the month.
67. The old gum under the desk could not spoil it.

### 第 7 段（16 句）
68. Shortly after the article appeared, the police arrested three people.
69. One was a former employee of the data company; the others were a pair of students who had bought the phishing kit and used it for a month.
70. The main suspect was held as a prisoner while the case was prepared.
71. The law, the officer said, would punish them, but it could not restore everything.
72. The university agreed to cover Xiao Lin's loss, and the officer suggested a fund for other victims.
73. A score of students had lost money, and none of them had been careless; they had simply trusted the wrong page.
74. Some looked at the recovered money with envy, but most felt relief.
75. At the meeting where the fund was announced, Xiao Lin stood up and thanked the team.
76. Her gratitude was quiet and complete.
77. "I will not be timid again," she said.
78. "I will ask questions." The officer called their work outstanding, and David felt the whole room warm toward her.
79. The decision to refund her was announced as soon as possible, and the police resumed their investigation with new evidence.
80. They reached an understanding with the university about the fund.
81. It was not a selfish crime; it hurt everyone.
82. There was no magic in the recovery, only a long, thorough process, and a little luck.
83. The only sin, David thought, was to blame the victim.

### 第 8 段（7 句）
84. The summer that followed felt like an adventure, though not a pleasant one.
85. The team launched a security guide, held an oral workshop, and traveled to other campuses to share what they had learned.
86. They discovered that most attacks began with a single click, and they encouraged students to ask before they clicked; people became more aware of the danger.
87. They did not pretend that the problem was solved; they knew that scammers compete with every defense, and that the battle would continue.
88. Their resources were limited, and they had little leisure, but the guide was a fantastic tool that benefited thousands of students, and they set a new standard for campus security.
89. He also understood that a reputation is built slowly and can be damaged in a single afternoon.
90. On the last evening of June, he sat on the sofa in the dorm and looked at the campus through the window.

### 第 9 段（9 句）
91. They had stayed indoors during the hottest hours, but now the air was cool.
92. The shade of the trees was deep, an insect crossed the glass, and somewhere a radio played an old song.
93. A tube of glue and a strip of tape lay on the table beside a cloth bag, and the milk in the fridge had gone sour.
94. He made a meal of meat and rice and read a poster about nutrition while he ate.
95. None of it was extraordinary, and all of it was worth protecting.
96. The city museum had opened an exhibit on the history of money, with a model of an old palace and a historic collection of coins behind crystal, but the real lesson was here.
97. It felt less like destiny than like a warning.
98. He picked up a pen and began to write the first draft of the guide.
99. Trust, he wrote, is not lost forever; it is rebuilt one careful check at a time.

## 写作规范（务必照做，全 30 章保持一致）

### 逐句译文
- 一句英文对一句中文，数组顺序 = 下面「逐句译文」里的编号顺序，**不能增删、不能换位**。
- 忠实、自然、文学化的中文；不要逐词硬译，句子要读得通。
- **人名地名一律音译，和第 1 章保持一致**：
  David→大卫，Mark→马克，Sarah→萨拉，Haizhou→海州，Haizhou University→海州大学，
  Lin Yuan（主角本名）→林远，Xiao Han→小韩，其它专有名词同理（拿不准就音译）。
  院校/机构名可保留英文（University of Chicago 等），但**人名必须音译**。
- **中文引号用弯引号 “ ”**（不要用「」或『』），破折号用 ——（两个 U+2014）。
- 数字/时间按中文习惯写：「七点十分」「九月第一个星期日」「四楼」「两个人」。
- 口语对白照原样译成引号里的中文；引号外的大段叙述用正常标点。
- 长句可以在语义转折处断成两句，但**不能改变句子数量**（一句话仍然对应数组里的一个元素）。
- 不要加解释、不要加括号注释、不要在译文里补充原文没有的信息。

### 词汇例句
- 每个词条一行，`.w` 的 `ex` 数组长度 = 该词在词表里的**行数**（一个词性一行）。
  例：`accord` 在词表里占了 2 行（n. 和 v.），那 `ex` 就要 2 个例句，第 1 个对应 n.，第 2 个对应 v.。
- `ipa`：给出音标，格式 `/ˈwɜːd/`，用国际音标，重音用 ˈ 或 ˌ，卷舌用 ː，长音用 ː，美音英音都行但要准确。
- 例句要**贴着该词性那个义项**写，不要一个例子糊弄所有词性。
- 例句是**可迁移的通用句**（不是照抄正文原句），CET4 难度，能体现这个词的典型用法。
- `cn` 是例句的中文翻译，要地道。
- `note` 可选：写用法提示或搭配（如 `abandon oneself to 沉溺于`、`in accord with 与…一致`）。没有特别值得写的就省略这个键。
- 例句里该词本身可以用别的形态（abandoned、abandoning、abandons 都行）。

### 产出格式
只写两个文件，不要改别的：
1. `content/chNN.sentences.json`：`{ "sentences": [ "...", ... ] }`
2. `content/chNN.vocab.json`：`{ "words": { "word": { "ipa": "/.../", "ex": [ { "en": "...", "cn": "...", "note": "..." } ] } } }`

- `words` 的键是**小写单词**；词表里写成 `a/b` 形式的就照抄（如 `exam/examination`、`defense/defence`）。
- 词表里同一个词出现多行时，只在 `words` 里出现**一次**，它的 `ex` 数组按行序放多个例句。
- JSON 用 UTF-8，不要有注释、不要有尾逗号。
- 写完必须自己校验：JSON 能 parse、sentences 数组长度 = 句子数、每个词的 ex 数量 = 它的词表行数。
