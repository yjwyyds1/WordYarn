### 第 1 段（12 句）
1. December arrived with a hard frost and a problem.
2. Shelf had six hundred users and a healthy list of trades, but the textbook season was over, and the numbers on Mark's screen had begun to go down.
3. The negative figures were an economic signal none of them liked; the only positive sign was that users came back.
4. They met in the borrowed office above a bar near the north gate.
5. Mark was restless, tapping a pen against the table; Sarah was frank as always.
6. "We built a market for a single week of the year," she said.
7. "In June and December we are useful. The rest of the time we are a notice board." David, impatient, wanted to argue, but he read the graph on the blackboard behind her and could not.
8. It rose in September and fell in November, a hill of demand with a long empty plain on either side.
9. A similar hill appeared every year, and when they compared the data, the pattern was identical.
10. Their intention had been good, but the business was not.
11. Nobody spoke for a while.
12. Then Mark said, "So we pivot. Or we die."

### 第 2 段（8 句）
13. The next morning David took the broken kettle to the electrical workshop behind the engineering building.
14. It was a century-old brick shed, heavy with the smell of oil and iron, and it was run by Mr. Zhou, a retired technician with a cheerful face and strict hands.
15. On his bench lay a soldering iron, a coil of wire, a bottle of acid, and the aluminium case of an old radio.
16. The workshop was full of old machinery.
17. A colony of ants lived under the bench, and a mosquito hummed near the lamp.
18. David ducked under a low pipe and set the kettle down.
19. "The problem is not the switch," Mr. Zhou said.
20. "It is the circuit. One small pipe of wire has burned through, and the rest of the machine is fine.

### 第 3 段（4 句）
21. The case is only plastic, made from petroleum, but the circuit is the heart." He looked at David over his glasses. "Most things fail like that.
22. Not all at once.
23. They give you hints first, and if you are not listening, they give you a disaster." The acid was a poison, he added, and had to be kept locked away. He stayed for an hour while the old man talked about his generation, the famine years, and the hardship of building a factory from nothing. "Under socialism we had little, but we shared it," he said. "Innovation is not a new idea.
24. It is an old idea that has finally found its moment." Beyond the window, the river lay low below the dam, the color of metal in the winter light.

32. They recruited twelve applicants in three days; the most promising was a first-year student named Li, who had a quick smile and a talent for sorting books.
33. "We will run a small camp at the shore on Saturday," Sarah said.
34. "Training, food, and a walk. It will build the team." They loaded the first cart from the trunk of an old car behind the dorm and pushed it down the ramp.

### 第 5 段（12 句）
35. The pilot began in the last week of term.
36. It was an immediate delight to the students who used it, and an immediate headache to David.
37. To his horror, someone tried to abuse the system by keeping a book for three months without paying; another left a carton of rubbish inside a locker.
38. The committee wanted to cancel the whole thing, and David could not bear the thought of failure.
39. He was upset, but he kept his judgment calm.
40. They added a guideline to the user agreement, a small penalty for late returns, and a strict rule about damage.
41. "We should not be cruel about one offense," he said.
42. "But there is no excuse for ignoring it either." The cart's handle struck his knee as they moved the lockers.
43. Two days later they wrapped the damaged books in brown paper and discarded two that were beyond repair.
44. Meantime, the campus clinic reported a rise in flu cases, and suddenly half the volunteers were ill.
45. Desperate for help, David called Li, who arrived with extensive notes and three friends.
46. The old radiator in the office gave up entirely, and they worked in coats, their breath visible in the cold.

### 第 6 段（13 句）
47. Then came the annual provincial student business competition, held that December in the city's tallest skyscraper.
48. Forty teams applied; sixteen passed.
49. Shelf would represent the university, and their rivals included a team from the agriculture college, with a cardboard cow and a plan to breed a better goat.
50. Their pitch was funny, but weak on numbers.
51. David's pulse quickened as he took the microphone, and his mind wandered to the cold office.
52. He presented the rental circuit on a bar chart: student to locker, locker to cart, cart to store, store back to student.
53. Sarah quoted a line from their own users: "I saved eighty yuan and two hours of shopping." The judges asked about corporate competition and whether the model could survive a variation in textbook prices.
54. "Suppose paper becomes expensive," one said.
55. "What then?" David answered with the graph and a frank admission that the first version had failed.
56. "We are not selling software," he said.
57. "We are selling trust, and trust is slower." A real concern, he admitted, was the cost of transport relative to the price of a book.
58. The head judge, a sympathetic woman from a state bank, nodded, and the question prompted a long discussion.
59. They took second place and a small grant, and Mr. Zhou, who had come to watch, whistled through his teeth with delight.

### 第 7 段（8 句）
60. Afterwards the team ate a feast in a small restaurant near the venue: soup, noodles, and a heavy plate of meat that Mark claimed was goat.
61. "My family raises sheep and cows," he said, "so I know a goat when I taste one." A film about missiles and spies played on the television above the bar, and nobody paid it much attention.
62. Nobody was thirsty for long, and the laughter was loud enough to disturb the next table.
63. A foreign student from the astronomy club joined them and talked about a satellite that would pass over the city that night.
64. "You can see it with the naked eye if the sky is clear," she said.
65. "It looks like it will float across the stars." Outside, the air was cold and clean, and above the skyscraper the sky was full of stars.
66. He thought of Mr. Zhou's phrase about the moment, and for the first time in weeks his outlook felt optimistic.
67. The diet of noodles and worry had worn him down.

### 第 8 段（9 句）
68. In the new term Shelf changed shape again.
69. They painted the lockers a bright yellow, printed a small map on each one, and posted a simple spelling of the rules in every dorm: reserve, collect, return.
70. An arrow on the wall pointed to the nearest locker, and a note asked users to avoid leaving rubbish inside.
71. The system was still small, and it was virtually impossible to predict demand, but the variation from week to week was manageable, and the steady numbers were an indication that the model worked.
72. Li took over the daily log and the volunteer schedule, and David began to teach him the things he had learned from Mr. Zhou: listen for the hint, check the circuit, respect the machine.
73. "You will make mistakes," he told him.
74. "I made numerous ones. The trick is to leave a record so the next person does not repeat them." Li wrote it all down, and David felt the quiet pleasure of passing on what he knew.
75. Several volunteers asked to continue after the term ended, and Li, likewise, began to train two first-year students of his own.
76. David preferred the quiet mornings alone.

### 第 9 段（9 句）
77. By the end of the month the numbers had risen again.
78. It was not a boom, and David was under no illusion that they had solved anything permanent.
79. But the business now had an outlook, and the team had a purpose beyond survival.
80. At the campus spring fair, he stood at the top of the library stairs and watched students carry yellow books across the square.
81. A scientist from the physics department stopped to ask how the lockers worked, and David explained the whole circuit with the confidence of a teacher.
82. He seldom talked about himself, but he was proud.
83. In the meantime, Mark had painted a small sign for the office door, and Sarah had printed a new set of cards.
84. The three of them had started with nothing but a problem and a notebook, and they were still together.
85. That, David thought, was the real prize: not the medal, but the habit of beginning again.

## 写作规范（务必照做，全 30 章保持一致）

### 逐句译文
- 一句英文对一句中文，数组顺序 = 下面「逐句译文」里的编号顺序，**不能增删、不能换位**。
- 忠实、自然、文学化的中文；不要逐词硬译，句子要读得通。
- **人名地名一律音译，和第 1 章保持一致**：
  David→大卫，Mark→马克，Sarah→萨拉，Haizhou→海州，Haizhou University→海州大学，
  Lin Yuan（主角本名）→林远，Xiao Han→小韩，其它专有名词同理（拿不准就音译）。
  院校/机构名可保留英文（University of Chicago 等），但**人名必须音译**。
- **中文引号用弯引号 “ ”**（不要用「」或『』），破折号用 ——（两个 U+2014）。
- 数字/时间按中文习惯写：「七点十分」「九月第一个星期日」「四楼」「两个人」。
- 口语对白照原样译成引号里的中文；引号外的大段叙述用正常标点。
- 长句可以在语义转折处断成两句，但**不能改变句子数量**（一句话仍然对应数组里的一个元素）。
- 不要加解释、不要加括号注释、不要在译文里补充原文没有的信息。

### 词汇例句
- 每个词条一行，`.w` 的 `ex` 数组长度 = 该词在词表里的**行数**（一个词性一行）。
  例：`accord` 在词表里占了 2 行（n. 和 v.），那 `ex` 就要 2 个例句，第 1 个对应 n.，第 2 个对应 v.。
- `ipa`：给出音标，格式 `/ˈwɜːd/`，用国际音标，重音用 ˈ 或 ˌ，卷舌用 ː，长音用 ː，美音英音都行但要准确。
- 例句要**贴着该词性那个义项**写，不要一个例子糊弄所有词性。
- 例句是**可迁移的通用句**（不是照抄正文原句），CET4 难度，能体现这个词的典型用法。
- `cn` 是例句的中文翻译，要地道。
- `note` 可选：写用法提示或搭配（如 `abandon oneself to 沉溺于`、`in accord with 与…一致`）。没有特别值得写的就省略这个键。
- 例句里该词本身可以用别的形态（abandoned、abandoning、abandons 都行）。

### 产出格式
只写两个文件，不要改别的：
1. `content/chNN.sentences.json`：`{ "sentences": [ "...", ... ] }`
2. `content/chNN.vocab.json`：`{ "words": { "word": { "ipa": "/.../", "ex": [ { "en": "...", "cn": "...", "note": "..." } ] } } }`

- `words` 的键是**小写单词**；词表里写成 `a/b` 形式的就照抄（如 `exam/examination`、`defense/defence`）。
- 词表里同一个词出现多行时，只在 `words` 里出现**一次**，它的 `ex` 数组按行序放多个例句。
- JSON 用 UTF-8，不要有注释、不要有尾逗号。
- 写完必须自己校验：JSON 能 parse、sentences 数组长度 = 句子数、每个词的 ex 数量 = 它的词表行数。
