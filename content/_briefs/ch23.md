## 一、逐句译文（按编号顺序，数组元素与编号一一对应）

### 第 1 段（7 句）
1. The history professor gave the assignment on a gray Monday: each student would choose one object from the Haizhou Museum and write a report of fifteen hundred words, due on Friday.
2. "Do not describe the object," she said.
3. "Follow it. Ask what it meant to the people who made it, and what it means now." David felt a familiar anxiety; he preferred machines, where every part had a function.
4. The requirement was clear; the museum was free.
5. On Wednesday he walked with Sarah and Mark across the lawn to the old building at the city's southern edge.
6. It had been a castle, then a gym, now a museum; its gray stone walls had been repaired until old and new blended into history.
7. A bronze lion stood beside the gate, and a banner carried an advertisement for a new exhibition.

### 第 2 段（9 句）
8. Inside, during the quiet morning, the hall was dark and cool, appropriate for fragile things.
9. An elevator carried them to the second floor, where "Objects of the Everyday" filled a series of rooms.
10. The collection was magnificent in an unexpected way: not crowns, but pans, forks, a chess set, a medal, a toy, a bowl with a painted flower.
11. The exhibition argued for equality: a king's cup and a farmer's fork deserved equal care.
12. He walked past oil paintings, a stone stamp and a length of rope, and looked over his shoulder.
13. Each object had a tag written in a code only the staff understood.
14. Then he saw it: a bronze needle, no longer than his little finger, green with age, lying on dark cloth.
15. The label was brief: "Bronze needle, c. 200 BCE." He stared at it for a long time.
16. It had an eye at one end and a point at the other, and it seemed to hold a story the label did not tell.

### 第 3 段（12 句）
17. An elderly visitor in a gray coat stood beside him, leaning on a walking stick.
18. "That is not a sewing needle," he said.
19. "I was a doctor for forty years. Look at the shaft. That is a medical needle." David looked again.
20. The man's certainty was strong, though David lacked knowledge to judge.
21. A guide came over, polite but firm.
22. "The catalogue is quite clear," she said.
23. The old man made a civil objection: he did not wish to impose his opinion, but he had seen such instruments in a medical museum in the north.
24. He had flown in from the airport after reading about the exhibition online.
25. The guide's smile became slightly awkward, and she did not insist.
26. David wrote the phrase "medical needle" in his notebook.
27. It seemed weird that a single word could change an object's identity.
28. He began to perceive a question worth following: whose voice decides what a thing is?

### 第 4 段（9 句）
29. In the library he borrowed three books on the intermediate period, including a rare first edition from 1928.
30. The discussion of burial objects was detailed; one chapter described a tomb in the south where a bronze needle had been found beside a pan, a fork, and a clay chess piece.
31. The site had been a farm, and the objects had lain under a barn for two thousand years, buried under ash from a fire.
32. David measured the drawing's geometry and calculated the proportion of the shaft to the eye.
33. He made notes slowly, needing time to digest it.
34. The book explained the usage of such tools in surgery, and the finding fit the old doctor's claim.
35. He took the notes to the physics lab, where a friend helped arrange an X-ray scan with the museum's permission.
36. The image showed a hollow channel inside the shaft, a detail no sewing needle would need.
37. The density was consistent with an instrument for delicate work.

### 第 5 段（8 句）
38. Ms. Wu, the museum's curator, was a considerate woman with a quick smile.
39. "You may be right," she said.
40. "Our records are not perfect." The museum had a small staff, a slim budget, and an endless storage room full of uncatalogued junk.
41. A porter dragged a crate across the floor; a loose label fell, and a young woman followed with a rag to wash the shelves.
42. The cost of conservation was high; humidity control alone consumed a third of the budget.
43. The chairman was retiring, and the museum's financial support was uncertain.
44. "It is a privilege to care for these objects," Ms. Wu said, "but an object's identity is not a small thing. If we are wrong, we should say so." She gave David a tip: the old doctor might know more; his name was on a visitor card.
45. David asked whether anyone had tried to rob the collection; she said the guards were better than the locks.

### 第 6 段（9 句）
46. The examination was scheduled for noon on Thursday.
47. In the lab, a technician in a white coat placed the needle on a clean surface and adjusted the X-ray machine.
48. The image showed a fine channel and a beveled tip.
49. "It is a surgical needle," the technician said.
50. "Probably for draining fluid. The eye is not for thread; it is for a handle." David's mobile buzzed, but he ignored it.
51. He had no motive except curiosity, and for once curiosity was enough.
52. He called the old doctor, who laughed with pleasure and said, "I told you." Ms. Wu agreed to declare the old label wrong and to note the finding in the next edition of the catalogue.
53. She invited him to write a short piece for the newsletter.
54. The discovery changed the way he saw the museum: not a warehouse of facts, but a place where facts were still being made.

### 第 7 段（10 句）
55. On Thursday afternoon a visitor fainted in the dark hall.
56. There was a scream, then a crowd, then shouting for help.
57. David ran for the staff; an ambulance arrived within the hour.
58. The woman was dizzy and pale, but the paramedics said it was nothing serious.
59. It was evident that she had skipped lunch.
60. The staff brought water and a chair; a married couple nearby held hands.
61. "For goodness' sake," an old man said, "give her some air." Later, Ms. Wu told him the museum should have a place to sit and rest.
62. "That is a requirement, not a luxury," she said.
63. It was an act of kindness, and it calmed the room.
64. The incident relieved David's anxiety about his own report; a museum, like a person, had nerves and needs.

### 第 8 段（11 句）
65. On Friday he submitted the report.
66. He had condensed forty pages of notes into eight, cutting every sentence without weight.
67. His first attempt had been too long, and in his haste he had repeated himself.
68. Sarah read the draft and gave him honest feedback: his prepositions were sometimes wrong, and his paragraphs were too long.
69. "You write like a machine," she said, and then, seeing his face, "a careful machine." Mark made sausage sandwiches while David revised, and the radio played a tune neither of them knew.
70. At midnight David was still awake, pasting a photograph of the needle onto the final page.
71. He wondered whether the professor would think the report suitable.
72. He felt crazy for caring so much about a needle.
73. A week later at the seminar she called it the best and named him a candidate for a departmental prize.
74. The prize was competitive, but in the long run the acceptance of his work meant more than the medal.
75. It was inevitable that he would compare himself to others.

### 第 9 段（12 句）
76. The museum held a night event as a farewell to the exhibition.
77. An orchestra played, and a young singer performed a song about the river; later the audience began to sing the chorus, and the music echoed off the stone walls.
78. A television crew filmed the crowd.
79. The museum itself seemed to breathe.
80. The exhibition had been a hit, and tourism to the museum had risen by a third.
81. People had come from across the city, and the shop sold more postcards than ever.
82. He made a small purchase: a book about the museum's history.
83. He walked through the rooms with Sarah, past families and their children, and felt a strange pride.
84. The needle was still there, but its label now read "Bronze surgical needle," with a short note explaining the correction.
85. A child stared at it and asked, "Did it hurt?" Her mother laughed.
86. He reflected on the object's long existence: made by a craftsman, buried in soil, found by accident, mislabeled, finally understood.
87. Its journey was a kind of venture, and the museum was its harbor.

### 第 10 段（13 句）
88. On the walk home, Sarah asked whether he would become a historian.
89. David said he did not know; engineering was still his field.
90. But he had gained something from the assignment: a habit of asking who had written the label, and why.
91. "History is not a list," he said.
92. "It is a discussion that never ends." Sarah nodded.
93. "Shall we go to the night market?" she asked.
94. Above them the clouds had cleared, and a planet shone beside the moon.
95. He thought of the old doctor, the curator and the technician; each had added a detail to the story.
96. Reality, he saw, was more complicated than the label, and the spiritual value of a thing could outlast its price.
97. The past was not a locked room but a door, and every generation turned the handle.
98. He wanted to cultivate that curiosity, and he would not surrender it to certainty.
99. Undoubtedly the museum would change again, and the needle would outlast them all.
100. For the first time, he felt at home in a museum; the old stone building seemed less like a treasure house and more like a conversation.

## 写作规范（务必照做，全 30 章保持一致）

### 逐句译文
- 一句英文对一句中文，数组顺序 = 下面「逐句译文」里的编号顺序，**不能增删、不能换位**。
- 忠实、自然、文学化的中文；不要逐词硬译，句子要读得通。
- **人名地名一律音译，和第 1 章保持一致**：
  David→大卫，Mark→马克，Sarah→萨拉，Haizhou→海州，Haizhou University→海州大学，
  Lin Yuan（主角本名）→林远，Xiao Han→小韩，其它专有名词同理（拿不准就音译）。
  院校/机构名可保留英文（University of Chicago 等），但**人名必须音译**。
- **中文引号用弯引号 “ ”**（不要用「」或『』），破折号用 ——（两个 U+2014）。
- 数字/时间按中文习惯写：「七点十分」「九月第一个星期日」「四楼」「两个人」。
- 口语对白照原样译成引号里的中文；引号外的大段叙述用正常标点。
- 长句可以在语义转折处断成两句，但**不能改变句子数量**（一句话仍然对应数组里的一个元素）。
- 不要加解释、不要加括号注释、不要在译文里补充原文没有的信息。

### 词汇例句
- 每个词条一行，`.w` 的 `ex` 数组长度 = 该词在词表里的**行数**（一个词性一行）。
  例：`accord` 在词表里占了 2 行（n. 和 v.），那 `ex` 就要 2 个例句，第 1 个对应 n.，第 2 个对应 v.。
- `ipa`：给出音标，格式 `/ˈwɜːd/`，用国际音标，重音用 ˈ 或 ˌ，卷舌用 ː，长音用 ː，美音英音都行但要准确。
- 例句要**贴着该词性那个义项**写，不要一个例子糊弄所有词性。
- 例句是**可迁移的通用句**（不是照抄正文原句），CET4 难度，能体现这个词的典型用法。
- `cn` 是例句的中文翻译，要地道。
- `note` 可选：写用法提示或搭配（如 `abandon oneself to 沉溺于`、`in accord with 与…一致`）。没有特别值得写的就省略这个键。
- 例句里该词本身可以用别的形态（abandoned、abandoning、abandons 都行）。

### 产出格式
只写两个文件，不要改别的：
1. `content/chNN.sentences.json`：`{ "sentences": [ "...", ... ] }`
2. `content/chNN.vocab.json`：`{ "words": { "word": { "ipa": "/.../", "ex": [ { "en": "...", "cn": "...", "note": "..." } ] } } }`

- `words` 的键是**小写单词**；词表里写成 `a/b` 形式的就照抄（如 `exam/examination`、`defense/defence`）。
- 词表里同一个词出现多行时，只在 `words` 里出现**一次**，它的 `ex` 数组按行序放多个例句。
- JSON 用 UTF-8，不要有注释、不要有尾逗号。
- 写完必须自己校验：JSON 能 parse、sentences 数组长度 = 句子数、每个词的 ex 数量 = 它的词表行数。
