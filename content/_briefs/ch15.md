## 一、逐句译文（按编号顺序，数组元素与编号一一对应）

### 第 1 段（11 句）
1. David came back to Haizhou on the first Monday of October with a notebook full of figures and a head full of plans.
2. The train from Beijing had arrived at dawn, and the morning air on the platform was cold and dry.
3. He had hardly slept, but the noise of the station did not annoy him.
4. The bus followed the bank of the river, and many of the passengers were students.
5. It was the most hopeful morning he had ever spent in the city.
6. His family had sent him back with a jar of jam and a warning to eat properly.
7. He had spent the summer at a software company, where he learned how a small team turns an idea into a product.
8. On the journey home he had filled twelve pages with notes about a problem at Haizhou University: students had no sensible way to buy and sell used textbooks.
9. The trade ran through a crude wall of paper notices near the canteen, where half the advertisements were out of date.
10. He was not sure the rules of the market were applicable to a campus, but the idea would not leave him alone.
11. He climbed four flights of stairs, tightened his belt against his hunger, and dropped his bag on the desk.

### 第 2 段（7 句）
12. Mark was still in bed under a poster of an astronaut, and he listened with one eye open.
13. David spoke with such intensity that Mark sat up and laughed at himself.
14. "You want to build a virtual shelf," Mark said, "a place where every book on campus has a number and a price." David nodded and showed him the outline.
15. He was altogether too excited to sit down.
16. "The concept is sound," Mark said, "but the technical side is not trivial. You need a database, a payment system, and someone who can repair the machine when it breaks. Fortunately, you know an undergraduate in computer science with free time." Sarah, when they found her in the library, was harder to persuade.
17. She pressed the notebook to her breast and read the outline twice, then raised one doubtful eyebrow.
18. "The idea is feasible," she said at last, "but only if you solve the cold start. Nobody wants to go shopping on an empty shelf."

### 第 3 段（10 句）
19. They made an agreement that afternoon at a corner table in the student center.
20. David would handle operations, Mark the technical work, and Sarah marketing.
21. They adopted the name Shelf because it was easy to spell.
22. Their first goal was modest: two hundred books before the end of term.
23. Mark wrote the code on a silver laptop whose hard disc made a horrible noise, and David carried boxes of old books from the library's extension as test data.
24. The first prototype was crude but worked: a student could photograph a book with a camera, type a price, and post it in a minute.
25. It could generate a list of comparable titles and sort them by price, which felt almost scientific.
26. The model had three variables: price, condition, and distance.
27. A comparative study of the canteen wall showed that the average asking price was thirty yuan.
28. In a lecture on clean energy, while the professor talked about hydrogen and space flight, David drew the logo in the margin with a red pen: three books standing upright, like a small shelf.

### 第 4 段（9 句）
29. The student affairs bureau ran a startup competition every autumn, and Mark insisted that they enter.
30. The pitch was held in the main lecture hall before a judge from a local company, two professors, and a woman from the commerce district office.
31. David's forehead was damp as he walked to the microphone, and his hands were stiff around his notes.
32. He spoke about the abundant supply of old books, the employment it could create, and the impact on the campus ecology.
33. "Every year we throw away books that are still ripe for a second reader," he said.
34. "Shelf gives them a second life." A judge asked what would happen when a big corporation copied them.
35. Sarah answered at once: "They can copy the software, but not the trust of a closed campus network. That is our exclusive advantage." The judge indicated with a nod that he liked the answer.
36. They won second prize, five thousand yuan.
37. The money entitled them to a small office, and David felt a quiet sense of achievement, though he was conscious that the hardest part had not begun.

### 第 5 段（13 句）
38. They launched on a cold Saturday in November in the central square opposite the library.
39. Mark had printed two hundred flyers held with rubber bands, Sarah had borrowed a table and made a poster to promote the launch, and a friend from the music club played a trumpet to draw a crowd.
40. They handed out sandwiches and coffee made from dark beans.
41. Sarah brought grapes so ripe that the juice ran down her wrist, and Mark, who could not eat dairy and had a sweet tooth, traded his cheese sandwich for cake.
42. A circle of children gathered around a kite above the square, and for an hour the scene felt like a festival, a rare piece of recreation.
43. The air smelled of petrol from a passing scooter.
44. A cheer went up when the first listing appeared on the screen.
45. Then black clouds gathered and a sudden spell of rain swept across the square.
46. "I am sorry," David said, and everyone seized the books and ran.
47. He nearly had a collision with a bicycle as he pulled the handcart toward the entrance.
48. A coffee stain spread across a textbook cover, and the electric kettle began to hiss in the rain.
49. David sent a text to Sarah: the books were safe.
50. By midnight, seventy students had signed up and thirty books had been posted, and their thirst was quenched with cold tea in the dorm.

### 第 6 段（15 句）
51. The second week was painful.
52. The number of listings flattened, and then it fell.
53. For a stretch of three weeks, Shelf sold fewer than ten books.
54. The continual silence on the site made David restless, and an inward doubt began to grow in him.
55. Had they deceived themselves?
56. The market was likely to be smaller than they had assumed, and they had no means to buy advertising.
57. Mark, who had a quiz that morning, wanted to spend their prize money on posters, but Sarah refused.
58. "We are guessing," she said.
59. "We need to ask." They interviewed twenty students in the canteen and learned something obvious: most people did not trust a stranger's description of a book's condition.
60. One user lodged a complaint about a missing disc, though it turned out to be an innocent mistake.
61. An anonymous message on the campus forum called them naive and predicted that Shelf would die before the new year.
62. The reaction of their earliest users surprised David: several defended the site in the same thread.
63. The harsh words wounded him, and he did not want to offend the people who had believed in them.
64. He went to bed without dinner, and in the dark he whispered to Mark, "Maybe the idea is not noble enough to survive." Mark did not answer for a long time.
65. "Or maybe it is a good idea with a bad design," he said.

### 第 7 段（9 句）
66. They rebuilt the design around one principle: transparency.
67. Every listing had to show four photographs, a fixed condition grade, and the seller's real name, and every price had to be transparent.
68. They wrote a short guide for new users and set up a shelf point in each dormitory, where a volunteer could inspect a book before the buyer paid.
69. Sarah sewed a banner with a needle and thread and hung it between two trees.
70. They cleared the weeds from the base of the old notice wall, then held a mock funeral for it: a cardboard coffin, a few candles, and a speech by Mark full of wit.
71. The campus reporter wrote a short piece about the event, and the coverage brought more users in a week than their flyers had in a month.
72. The new framework of rules was simple enough for anyone to follow.
73. They invited the campus bookstore to a meeting, and after two hours the manager agreed to a small pilot.
74. Shelf would handle the store's second-hand stock, and the store would let them use a corner of its counter as a drop-off point.

### 第 8 段（11 句）
75. The pilot changed everything.
76. By the end of December, the number of active users had passed six hundred, and the site was handling thirty trades a week.
77. It was not heaven, and the money was still tight, but the trend was finally upward.
78. They held a small party in a restaurant near the north gate, with a delicious cake, and Sarah made a speech that was shorter than Mark's and much better.
79. "We are not pioneers of anything," she said.
80. "We are just the first people on this campus who asked a simple question and refused to guess the answer." A supporter from the bookstore called the platform a symbol of what students could do together.
81. It had become a social network as much as a market.
82. Shelf's position was still small, but it rested on mutual respect rather than clever tricks.
83. When a professor asked him lately what he had learned, he thought for a moment and said, "That a good relationship with your users is worth more than a clever algorithm." The professor nodded and pointed at the door.
84. "Then go and keep it," she said.
85. Outside, the winter sky was clear, and the shelf in the window looked as he had imagined it.

## 写作规范（务必照做，全 30 章保持一致）

### 逐句译文
- 一句英文对一句中文，数组顺序 = 下面「逐句译文」里的编号顺序，**不能增删、不能换位**。
- 忠实、自然、文学化的中文；不要逐词硬译，句子要读得通。
- **人名地名一律音译，和第 1 章保持一致**：
  David→大卫，Mark→马克，Sarah→萨拉，Haizhou→海州，Haizhou University→海州大学，
  Lin Yuan（主角本名）→林远，Xiao Han→小韩，其它专有名词同理（拿不准就音译）。
  院校/机构名可保留英文（University of Chicago 等），但**人名必须音译**。
- **中文引号用弯引号 “ ”**（不要用「」或『』），破折号用 ——（两个 U+2014）。
- 数字/时间按中文习惯写：「七点十分」「九月第一个星期日」「四楼」「两个人」。
- 口语对白照原样译成引号里的中文；引号外的大段叙述用正常标点。
- 长句可以在语义转折处断成两句，但**不能改变句子数量**（一句话仍然对应数组里的一个元素）。
- 不要加解释、不要加括号注释、不要在译文里补充原文没有的信息。

### 词汇例句
- 每个词条一行，`.w` 的 `ex` 数组长度 = 该词在词表里的**行数**（一个词性一行）。
  例：`accord` 在词表里占了 2 行（n. 和 v.），那 `ex` 就要 2 个例句，第 1 个对应 n.，第 2 个对应 v.。
- `ipa`：给出音标，格式 `/ˈwɜːd/`，用国际音标，重音用 ˈ 或 ˌ，卷舌用 ː，长音用 ː，美音英音都行但要准确。
- 例句要**贴着该词性那个义项**写，不要一个例子糊弄所有词性。
- 例句是**可迁移的通用句**（不是照抄正文原句），CET4 难度，能体现这个词的典型用法。
- `cn` 是例句的中文翻译，要地道。
- `note` 可选：写用法提示或搭配（如 `abandon oneself to 沉溺于`、`in accord with 与…一致`）。没有特别值得写的就省略这个键。
- 例句里该词本身可以用别的形态（abandoned、abandoning、abandons 都行）。

### 产出格式
只写两个文件，不要改别的：
1. `content/chNN.sentences.json`：`{ "sentences": [ "...", ... ] }`
2. `content/chNN.vocab.json`：`{ "words": { "word": { "ipa": "/.../", "ex": [ { "en": "...", "cn": "...", "note": "..." } ] } } }`

- `words` 的键是**小写单词**；词表里写成 `a/b` 形式的就照抄（如 `exam/examination`、`defense/defence`）。
- 词表里同一个词出现多行时，只在 `words` 里出现**一次**，它的 `ex` 数组按行序放多个例句。
- JSON 用 UTF-8，不要有注释、不要有尾逗号。
- 写完必须自己校验：JSON 能 parse、sentences 数组长度 = 句子数、每个词的 ex 数量 = 它的词表行数。
